## Receiver alignments

Wide receivers are commonly described by where they line up:

- X receiver — the split end, usually on the line of scrimmage and isolated on
  the weak side. Typically faces the opponent's best cornerback and must beat
  press coverage.
- Z receiver — the flanker, lined up off the line behind the tight end, free to
  use motion to avoid press.
- Slot receiver — aligned inside, between the tackle and the outside receiver.
  Sees more zone coverage, shorter routes, and higher target volume.

A player's alignment is one of the strongest predictors of his target profile.
Slot receivers accumulate receptions; X receivers accumulate air yards.

## Running back roles

- Bell-cow back — takes the majority of both carries and passing-down snaps.
- Committee or timeshare — two or more backs split work, usually reducing the
  fantasy value of each below the sum of the parts.
- Early-down back — plays on first and second down, usually leaves on obvious
  passing downs.
- Passing-down back — plays on third down and in two-minute situations,
  accumulating receptions rather than carries.
- Goal-line back — receives carries inside the five-yard line, a small role with
  outsized touchdown value.

## Tight end usage

Tight ends range from in-line blockers who rarely run routes to receiving tight
ends aligned in the slot on most snaps. The position label alone says very
little; route participation and snap share say a great deal.

## Quarterback rushing

A quarterback who runs frequently adds a scoring category unavailable to a
pocket passer. In fantasy terms this raises both the floor and the ceiling,
because rushing yardage is less dependent on the performance of receivers.

## Defensive terms

- Box — the area immediately behind the defensive line. A "stacked box" means
  eight or more defenders near the line, signalling a commitment to stopping the
  run.
- Nickel and dime — defensive packages with five and six defensive backs
  respectively, used against passing formations.
- Coverage shell — the alignment of deep safeties. Single-high means one deep
  safety, two-high means two.

## Offensive and defensive line

Line positions are left tackle, left guard, centre, right guard and right
tackle on offence, and edge, interior and nose positions on defence. Continuity
along the offensive line correlates with both pressure rate allowed and rushing
efficiency.
