## Touchdown

A touchdown is worth six points. It is scored when the ball is legally carried
into or caught within the opponent's end zone, or recovered there after a
fumble or a change of possession.

## Extra point and two-point conversion

After a touchdown the scoring team chooses one of two attempts from scrimmage:

- An extra point, a place kick worth one point, snapped from the 15-yard line.
- A two-point conversion, an offensive play from the 2-yard line worth two
  points if the ball reaches the end zone.

A defence that returns a turnover on either attempt to the opposite end zone
scores two points.

## Field goal

A field goal is worth three points. It is scored when a place kick or drop kick
passes above the crossbar and between the uprights.

## Safety

A safety is worth two points, awarded to the defending team when the ball
becomes dead in the offence's own end zone while the offence is responsible for
it being there. The team charged with the safety then kicks the ball back to
the scoring team.

## Fair catch kick

A rarely used rule allows a team that makes a fair catch to attempt an
unobstructed free kick field goal from the spot of the catch, worth three
points.

## Overtime

Regular-season overtime is a single ten-minute period. Both teams are
guaranteed a possession. If the score remains level after the period, the game
ends in a tie.

Postseason overtime uses fifteen-minute periods, both teams are guaranteed a
possession, and play continues until a team leads at the end of a period or
scores after both have possessed the ball. Postseason games cannot end in a
tie.

## Standings points

The NFL does not use a points system for standings. Teams are ranked by
win–loss–tie record, expressed as a winning percentage in which a tie counts as
half a win and half a loss.
