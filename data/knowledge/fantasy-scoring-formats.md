## Standard scoring

Standard scoring, sometimes called non-PPR, awards no points for receptions.
The common values are:

- Passing yards: 1 point per 25 yards
- Passing touchdown: 4 points
- Interception thrown: -2 points
- Rushing yards: 1 point per 10 yards
- Receiving yards: 1 point per 10 yards
- Rushing or receiving touchdown: 6 points
- Fumble lost: -2 points
- Two-point conversion scored or passed: 2 points

Standard scoring favours running backs who score touchdowns and receivers who
produce large yardage totals, because volume without yardage earns nothing.

## PPR scoring

PPR stands for "points per reception". Every catch is worth one additional
point on top of the standard values above.

PPR raises the value of high-volume, short-target receivers and pass-catching
running backs. A slot receiver with ten catches for sixty yards scores six
points in standard scoring and sixteen in PPR — the same game, nearly three
times the value.

## Half-PPR scoring

Half-PPR awards 0.5 points per reception. It is the most common format in
public leagues because it narrows the gap between the two extremes without
eliminating the reception bonus entirely.

## Superflex

A superflex league adds a roster slot that may be filled by a quarterback in
addition to the usual starting quarterback. Because most teams then start two
quarterbacks, quarterback scarcity rises sharply and quarterbacks are drafted
far earlier than in single-quarterback leagues.

Superflex is a roster rule, not a scoring rule: the points per passing yard do
not change, only how many quarterbacks a team may start.

## Tight end premium

Tight end premium, or TEP, awards tight ends additional points per reception —
commonly 0.5 above the league's base reception value. It exists to offset the
historically low scoring of the position relative to wide receivers.

## Why the format matters when comparing players

The same statistical line produces different fantasy totals in different
formats. Any comparison between two players is only meaningful once the scoring
format is stated. A running back who catches six passes a game and a running
back who catches one are much closer in standard scoring than in full PPR.
